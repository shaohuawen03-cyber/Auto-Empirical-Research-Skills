# Cover letter (v0.4 submission)

**To the Editor of [JOURNAL]**

Dear Editor,

Please consider our manuscript, “A proteome-supported screen of periodontitis-specific sORF peptides yields twelve metal-binding, barrier-passing candidates that dock to the pathogenic peripheral anionic site of acetylcholinesterase.”

Periodontitis is linked to Alzheimer’s disease, but most molecular papers stop at *Porphyromonas gingivalis* cells, gingipains or outer-membrane vesicles. We first asked a countable question: after a periodontitis-specific sORF library is restricted to peptides that oral proteomes have actually seen (PRJNA678453 / PRJEB65451; 296 MAGs; 24 healthy vs 26 periodontitis donors), how many survive stacked filters for predicted blood–brain-barrier passage, Cu/Fe/Zn binding, and a chelator-high / scavenger-low profile? The answer is 12 (8 at a stricter cut). We then took those twelve 7–9-residue, His/Cys-rich candidates to the structure level: PAS-focused AutoDock Vina docking against human AChE (PDB 4EY6) places every peptide on the PAS–gorge interface (−8.25 to −9.60 kcal/mol), the same surface that accelerates Aβ fibril assembly and neighbours the published Aβ residence patch (residues 344–361).

We are explicit about what the paper does not claim. Vina scores rank poses; they are not binding constants. A 100-ns MD check was attempted and is reported as excluded after pressure instabilities, with the remediation plan stated. No wet assays were performed: metal binding, Cu/Fe-dependent ROS, lipid peroxidation, cholinesterase activity, Aβ aggregation and neuronal viability form the stated validation cascade, with a stopping rule for any pro-oxidant neurotoxicity claim. We also flag a taxonomy boundary: the collection is a community oral metagenome, not a *P. gingivalis*-only peptidome.

We suggest this fits [JOURNAL] because it couples a methods-transparent computational triage (donor *n* = 24 vs 26 as the independent unit) with a structure-based readout on a disease-relevant site, and a falsifiable experimental roadmap.

The work has not been published or submitted elsewhere. The authors declare no competing interests.
