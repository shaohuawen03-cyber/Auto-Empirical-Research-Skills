# Cover letter (first submission)

**To the Editor of [JOURNAL]**

Dear Editor,

Please consider our manuscript, “A proteome-supported screen of periodontitis-specific sORF peptides yields twelve blood–brain-barrier and metal-binding candidates for Alzheimer-oriented follow-up.”

Periodontitis is linked to Alzheimer’s disease, but most molecular papers stop at *Porphyromonas gingivalis* cells or gingipain proteases. We asked a narrower question that can be answered with public assemblies and oral proteomes: after a periodontitis-specific sORF library is restricted to peptides that mass spectrometry has actually seen, how many survive stacked filters for predicted blood–brain-barrier passage, Cu/Fe/Zn binding, and a chelator-high / scavenger-low profile?

Using 296 MAGs from 24 healthy and 26 periodontitis donors (PRJNA678453 / PRJEB65451), we collapse 11.7 million periodontitis sORFs to 33,786 proteome-supported peptides and then to **12** (8) computational candidates. We do not claim that these peptides generate ROS or bind acetylcholinesterase. Docking and wet assays are stated as the next stage. We also flag a taxonomy boundary: the collection is a community oral metagenome, not a *P. gingivalis*-only peptidome.

We suggest this fits [JOURNAL] because it is a methods-transparent computational triage with an explicit independent-unit statement (donor *n* = 24 vs 26) and a synthesis-scale output.

The work has not been published or submitted elsewhere. The authors declare no competing interests.
